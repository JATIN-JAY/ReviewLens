SECTION 02 · THE CASE FOR AUTOMATION

When confidence
outpaces the evidence

The promise of algorithmic care is often told as a story of inevitability. Yet the evidence behind that promise is more conditional than the story suggests.

Health systems are under pressure to do more with less. In response, a growing number of hospitals have begun piloting tools that promise to move routine decisions from the clinic to the computer. The most ambitious projections describe a near-term transformation in how care is delivered, with efficiency gains that could reach every patient.

Early results appear encouraging. A 2023 evaluation across three hospital networks found that an automated triage assistant reduced average intake time by 18 percent. The report concludes these systems can return meaningful time to clinical teams, allowing staff to focus on complex cases.

But the pilots were conducted in well-resourced urban hospitals, supported by dedicated implementation teams. The same approach may look different in settings where data is less complete or where clinicians have fewer ways to challenge a recommendation. Few published evaluations follow outcomes beyond the first year.

“Performance in a controlled rollout tells us what a tool can do. It tells us less about what a health system can sustain.”— Center for Clinical Systems, 2024

The distinction matters. A faster intake process is a useful outcome, but it is not by itself evidence of safer or more equitable care. The available evaluations have not yet shown whether time saved translates into better patient outcomes.
